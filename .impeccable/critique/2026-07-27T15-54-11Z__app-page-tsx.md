#### Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment**: Not an obvious "AI made this" at first glance — no gradient text, no numbered eyebrow scaffolding, no identical card grids, no cream/sand default; genuine asymmetric bento/sticky-stack/horizontal-gallery layouts and a real bespoke signature motif (the Convergence Line). Real, above-average craft discipline.

Two things puncture it on inspection:

1. **Every image site-wide is `picsum.photos/seed/...`** — a random placeholder-photo service. The seed strings read like real art direction (`meridian-bay-skyline-hero`, `confluence-incentives-coast`) but Picsum's seed selects a deterministic *random* photo; it does not match keywords to content. Verified visually: the "Incentives" bento tile renders a baseball being held in someone's hands; "Exhibitions" renders a lake dock. This directly undercuts the project's own #1 design principle ("sell the destination first — the emotional case has to land before the logistics case").

2. **Fraunces / editorial-typographic lane**: this composition lands substantially inside the brand register's reflex-reject "editorial-typographic" lane. Present: a serif display face (Fraunces — explicitly named on the reflex-reject font list), small tracked mono labels used repeatedly as both stat captions *and*, in two places (`hero.tsx`'s "Meetings · Incentives · Conferences · Exhibitions" and `cta-band.tsx`'s "Ready when you are"), as pure decorative eyebrow copy, hairline rule dividers under nearly every stat block, and a restrained near-monochromatic surface with one accent. DESIGN.md's own text claims the mono label "is doing a real job... not decorating a section like a kicker would" — but those two spots contradict that stated rule. The differentiators (asymmetric structure, no italics, the Convergence Line) are real, but the typography and eyebrow usage genuinely sit in the saturated lane, not clearly outside it.

**Deterministic scan**: `detect.mjs --json app components public` → `[]`, exit 0. Zero rule hits. Take this with real caveat: the detector is an HTML/CSS pattern engine; this codebase is TSX + Tailwind utility classes, which is a weaker match for its rule surface, so a clean scan here carries less weight than on a plain-HTML project. No false positives to report since there were no positives.

**Browser evidence**: Confirmed directly (both sub-agents' sandboxes couldn't reach localhost — I gathered this myself after restarting the dev server). Desktop 1440×900 and mobile 390×844, full-page, scrolled to trigger all reveals: zero console errors or warnings on either viewport. Mobile layout reflows cleanly with no overflow. Visually confirms the picsum mismatch issue (see above) and confirms the responsive bento/gallery/stack sections all render as intended.

#### Overall Impression

The bones are genuinely good — a real signature motif, disciplined copy, asymmetric structure instead of template scaffolding, clean responsive behavior, zero console errors. The gap is between what DESIGN.md *says* the system does and what's actually shipped: the mono label is documented as "data only" but used decoratively twice; the card border+shadow rule is stated but violated once; "drag-to-scroll" is documented for the destinations gallery but not implemented. The single biggest opportunity is imagery — replacing randomly-seeded placeholder photos with real, matched photography would cash in the emotional positioning the copy is already earning.

#### What's Working

1. **The Convergence Line signature motif** — a real, restrained, three-scale system (hero draw-in, section divider, data connector), reduced-motion aware, never purely decorative. Mechanically tied to the brand concept rather than bolted on.
2. **Case-studies sticky-stack** — the `position: sticky` staggered stacking makes "Proof, stacked" a literal, felt mechanic instead of a metaphor sitting above generic cards.
3. **Copy discipline** — specific, declarative voice throughout ("48-hour hold, no penalty," named venues, real capacity numbers) that matches the stated brand personality and avoids filler enthusiasm.

#### Priority Issues

- **[P1] Placeholder imagery undermines the core positioning.** Every image (hero, bento tiles, destination cards, case-study photos, about) is `picsum.photos/seed/...` — random, not content-matched. This also means no image can carry honest descriptive alt text yet (they currently all ship `alt=""`, which is the *correct* choice for genuinely random photography, but is a symptom of the real problem, not a separate bug). **Fix**: replace with verified, keyword-matched photography (real destination shots or verified Unsplash IDs), then add real alt text once the images actually depict what the copy claims. **Suggested command**: `polish` (imagery pass) — deferred, needs the user's input on image sourcing before executing.
- **[P1] Reassurance is backloaded away from the highest-hesitation CTA.** The hero's "Request a Proposal" has no supporting microcopy; the only reassurance line on the page ("Five minutes... a shortlist and a named contact — not a brochure") sits in the final CTA band, after trust-building is already done. **Fix**: surface a short reassurance line near the hero CTA. **Suggested command**: `clarify`.
- **[P2] Mobile nav drawer has no Escape-key or backdrop dismiss.** Closes only via the X button or a route change. **Fix**: add an Escape-key listener and a tap-outside dismiss. **Suggested command**: `harden`.
- **[P2] Ghost-card border+shadow combo on case-study cards contradicts the project's own card rule.** `case-studies-stack.tsx:31` pairs a 1px border with a 50px-blur shadow — DESIGN.md states "never both." **Fix**: drop the border, keep the shadow (reinforces the "stacking, elevated" metaphor). **Suggested command**: `polish`.
- **[P2] Destinations gallery has no discoverable scroll affordance.** DESIGN.md specifies "drag-to-scroll on desktop"; the shipped code is a bare `overflow-x-auto` with no drag handler, cursor affordance, or position indicator — a documented spec that isn't actually implemented. **Fix**: add a visible scroll cue (position dots or a subtle edge fade) at minimum; consider real pointer-drag for desktop. **Suggested command**: `polish`.

#### Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (confused first-timer)**: "Request a Proposal" appears in the header and hero with no clarifying microcopy about what it commits her to — she only learns it's "five minutes... a shortlist and a named contact" if she scrolls to the very bottom CTA band.

**Riley (deliberate stress tester)**: DESIGN.md promises drag-to-scroll on the destinations gallery; the shipped code has no drag handler and no visible affordance that more cards exist off-screen — a real gap between documented spec and shipped code. The logo marquee items are plain `<span>`s with no links — "proof" that dead-ends with no way to verify any claim behind it.

**Casey (distracted mobile user)**: The header's "Request a Proposal" button is `hidden lg:block` — on mobile there's no persistent one-tap CTA; Casey must open the hamburger menu first, then find the CTA at the bottom of the slide-in panel.

#### Minor Observations

- Footer social links point to `href="#"` — dead placeholder links.
- Hero image container uses `rounded-3xl` (24px) while the stated card convention is `rounded-2xl` (16px) — a minor scale inconsistency, well under the 32px+ ban threshold.
- The logo marquee only pauses on hover, not `:focus-within` — does nothing for keyboard-focus users (low stakes; decorative).
- The mono `.label` class is reused for genuinely different jobs (data captions vs. decorative eyebrows vs. nav-column headers) without visual distinction between them.

#### Questions to Consider

- Is a randomly-seeded placeholder-photo service actually better than no photography here, or would a more honest "coming soon" treatment (one confirmed hero photo, deliberately abstract art for the rest) protect the brand more than stock that might contradict its own captions?
- DESIGN.md insists the mono label never decorates like a kicker — given it's doing exactly that in two spots, is the real intent to make the eyebrow a deliberate, named, consistent device (used everywhere), or to enforce the "data only" rule (and remove it from those two spots)? Right now it's neither.
- The best reassurance copy on the site is saved for the visitor who's already convinced — why not the one still deciding whether to click the first time?
