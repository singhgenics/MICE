# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Corporate HR heads, L&D leads, and internal event/travel managers who own the budget for annual offsites, incentive trips, leadership retreats, and executive meets. They are increasingly judged on Return on Engagement (ROE), not just logistics, and they carry ESG reporting requirements into vendor selection. They compare a small number of specialist operators before committing a headcount, budget band, and Q3/Q4 window. Secondary audience: company founders and leadership teams booking their own annual meet or a reward trip for top performers.

## Product Purpose

Frontier Tourism is the dedicated MICE (Meetings, Incentives, Conferences, Exhibitions) division of The Traveller Co., built and run separately from the company's leisure travel business. The site makes the business case for two named retreat programs (a Northeast India deep-focus track and a Southeast Asia scale track), shows a real, dated track record of delivered corporate trips, and converts a visitor's interest into a submitted brief. Success is a qualified proposal request: a planner who arrived comparing MICE operators leaves having sent a headcount, budget band, and objective, expecting a structural blueprint back within 48 hours.

## Positioning

Frontier Tourism designs purpose-driven MICE retreats, not generic leisure packages relabeled for a corporate group. The site exists to make that distinction unmistakable: this is a specialist B2B operator with its own delivery history, not the same desk that books honeymoons and family holidays.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: Request a Proposal (opens the event-brief form)
- Secondary CTA: Explore the Retreat Programs (for planners who haven't picked a track yet)
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: this operator has already run this exact kind of trip, for named companies, on named dates, and can show the receipts.
- Belief ladder: (1) MICE is genuinely a separate discipline from leisure travel, run by people who only do this, leading to (2) the two retreat tracks map cleanly onto a real corporate objective, not a generic itinerary, leading to (3) named companies have already run this exact kind of trip and said so in their own words, leading to (4) sending a brief costs five minutes and returns a real, dated, structural response within 48 hours.
- Proof on hand: real. Work-experience entries (OSK Group, Gajraj & Sons, La Favela, GlaxoSmithKline's Gujarat region team), a dated workflow history from 2020 to 2026, a 2025 to 2026 booking-share breakdown by destination, and three named client testimonials, all sourced from the operator's own pitch deck. Do not invent additional clients, quotes, or statistics beyond what the deck supplies.

## Brand Personality

Expedition-minded and quietly confident, not luxury-brochure smooth. The existing brand mark ("Frontier Tourism, Horizons are calling") is rugged and hand-cut, closer to a field dispatch than a five-star hotel ad. The site's voice should match that: specific about logistics and ESG the way a serious operator has to be, but never sanded down into generic hospitality copy. No filler enthusiasm ("elevate," "seamless," "unlock," "unleash"). Confidence comes from dated, named specifics, not adjectives.

## Anti-references

Not a generic leisure travel agency site (destination carousels, "book your dream vacation" copy, price-per-person badges). Not a cold enterprise SaaS template. Not a luxury hospitality brochure with no operational substance behind it. Not cream and sand as a default background. No em dashes anywhere in any copy, headings, or body text.

## Design Principles

- State the leisure-versus-MICE split explicitly rather than implying it. A visitor should know within the first screen that this is not a general travel agency.
- Every claim traces to something in the source deck: a named client, a dated trip, a real percentage. No invented statistics.
- The two retreat tracks (Northeast India, Southeast Asia) are the spine of the site's information architecture, not an afterthought under a generic "destinations" umbrella.
- The proposal form is never more than one click away, but never interrupts browsing with a modal or popup.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA as the floor: 4.5:1 body text contrast, visible keyboard focus states, full keyboard operability of nav, menus, and forms, `prefers-reduced-motion` alternatives for all scroll and hero motion, alt text on every meaningful image.
